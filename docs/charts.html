Charts

What each report chart is doing, the data behind it, and why it matters for budgeting.

#Dashboard At A Glance

  • Data used: four headline KPIs from the same report context, typically income, spending, cash flow, and savings rate.
  • Why it matters: this is your five-second health check before you dive into trend charts and category breakdowns.
  • What it tells you: whether your current period is net-positive, spending-heavy, or tracking toward a healthier savings posture.

#Spending by Category

  • Data used: monthly transaction totals grouped by category, stacked so each bar shows total spend composition.
  • Why it matters: this is your top-level budget map, showing where money goes and which categories dominate each month.

#Compare Years

  • Data used: the same category totals, pivoted so month or quarter buckets align across multiple years.
  • Why it matters: it makes season-over-season comparisons faster, such as Jan vs Jan or Q3 vs Q3, instead of eyeballing separate timelines.

#Focused Mode

  • Data used: one selected category remains emphasized while other categories are de-emphasized as context.
  • Why it matters: emphasis beats color noise when the question is specific, like what Food is doing over time.

#Spending Seasonality

  • Data used: category-by-month matrix, with each row shaded relative to its own low/high range.
  • Why it matters: it reveals timing patterns quickly, such as holiday spikes or summer travel bursts that are easy to miss in totals-only charts.

#Expense Volatility

  • Data used: monthly spread per category using min, max, and average values over the selected period.
  • Why it matters: volatile categories are budget-breakers, so this highlights where sinking-fund buffers are most valuable.

#Subscription & Recurring Audit

  • Data used: recurring merchants detected by cadence and consistency, annualized from monthly equivalents.
  • Why it matters: recurring spend can hide in plain sight; this view exposes total yearly drag and fast cancellation targets.

#Cash Flow

  • Data used: income and spending month-by-month, transformed into net movement over time.
  • Why it matters: this is your runway signal; it shows whether your month pattern compounds toward stability or burn.

#Fixed vs Variable Spending

  • Data used: categories mapped into baseline fixed costs and flexible variable costs, then stacked by month.
  • Why it matters: fixed spend defines your minimum operating cost, while variable spend shows where optimization and cuts are possible.
